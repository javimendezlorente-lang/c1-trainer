# Part 3 benchmark — Word formation

Status: Phase 8.5A baseline

## Official format rule

Cambridge defines a text with eight gaps. A prompt word is supplied for each and must be changed to form the missing word; each answer earns one mark. The focus includes affixation, internal changes and compounding. See the [official format page](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/advanced/format/) and [handbook](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/167804-c1-advanced-handbook.pdf).

## Observed official exemplars

The two official sample texts measured 146 and 128 running words excluding gap markers, about 16–18 words per gap. These are empirical measurements, not official word limits.

## Current corpus audit

The three seed texts contain 89, 57 and 76 words. All are materially shorter than the observed examples, and one is less than half their mean length.

The seed set also contains derivational concerns. For example, a supplied base and answer must belong to a defensible word family; pairings such as a base meaning “explain” with an answer meaning “explicit” require rejection, not reviewer interpretation. Roots that are already derived forms need deliberate justification.

Severity: **HIGH** for under-length; **CRITICAL** for any invalid base-to-answer derivation.

## Empirically derived internal target

- 125–170 running words with eight prompt words.
- A coherent titled text, not eight isolated derivation cues.
- Natural coverage of noun/verb/adjective/adverb changes, negation, internal change or compounding as the text permits.
- Unique required spelling and word class in context.
- Base-to-answer morphology must be explainable and auditable.

## Critic rubric and hard rejection

Reject a candidate when the answer is not genuinely derived from the displayed base, an unchanged base fits without a real task, multiple forms fit, the target is trivial below C1, spelling is disputed without accepted variants, or the reconstructed passage is unnatural.
