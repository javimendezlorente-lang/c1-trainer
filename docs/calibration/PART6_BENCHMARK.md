# Part 6 benchmark — Cross-text multiple matching

Status: Phase 8.5A baseline

## Official format rule

Cambridge defines four short texts followed by four cross-text multiple-matching questions, worth two marks each. The focus is understanding, comparing and contrasting opinions and attitudes across texts. See the [official format page](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/advanced/format/) and [handbook](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/167804-c1-advanced-handbook.pdf).

## Observed official exemplars

The two official four-text sets measured 535 and 617 running words in total, about 134–154 words per question. These are empirical sample measurements.

The important benchmark is not length alone: prompts relate one writer’s position to another or require fine distinctions among overlapping attitudes. Four independent “who mentions X?” lookups do not reproduce the construct.

## Current corpus audit

The current sets contain 163, 150 and 158 source words in total—roughly 27% of the observed mean. Most prompts ask which writer independently mentions one concern. They can be solved without comparing texts.

Severity: **CRITICAL** for length and **HIGH** for construct mismatch.

## Empirically derived internal target

- Four texts, 520–680 running words total.
- Four questions, two marks each.
- Overlapping topic and vocabulary, with distinct nuanced stances.
- A material proportion of prompts must require agreement, disagreement, qualification or comparison across writers.
- No one-to-one answer pattern or simple unique-keyword matching.

## Critic rubric and hard rejection

The critic must build a writer-by-claim matrix and test every competing writer. Reject if a prompt is a direct lexical lookup, two writers support the claim equally, stances are caricatures, or questions never require cross-text reasoning.
