# Part 8 benchmark — Multiple matching

Status: Phase 8.5A baseline

## Official format rule

Cambridge defines one text or several short texts with ten multiple-matching questions, one mark each. The focus includes detail, opinion, attitude and specific information, and a section may be selected more than once. See the [official format page](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/advanced/format/) and [handbook](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/167804-c1-advanced-handbook.pdf).

## Observed official exemplars

The two official sample tasks each used four labelled source sections and measured 693 and 655 running words, about 66–69 words per question. Four sections are an observed pattern, not an official universal requirement.

Questions paraphrase precise claims distributed through longer sections. Repeated selection of a section prevents a simple elimination strategy.

## Current corpus audit

The three seed sets contain 165, 149 and 174 source words—about 24% of the observed mean. Ten questions over four extremely short texts create high answer density and make lexical matching or elimination much easier than sustained scanning.

Severity: **CRITICAL** under-calibration.

## Empirically derived internal target

- 620–750 source words; four sections are the initial preferred blueprint but not claimed as an official rule.
- Exactly ten questions.
- Each section can support several answers; answer distribution is balanced without being mechanical.
- Prompts use genuine paraphrase and cover detail, opinion, attitude and specific information where the source supports them.
- Sections share topic vocabulary so unique-word matching is insufficient.

## Critic rubric and hard rejection

The critic must identify a unique evidence span and the nearest competing section for every prompt. Reject direct phrase copying, one-question-per-section patterns, obvious answer distribution, unsupported inference, two equally valid sections or insufficient source length for scanning demand.
